How to Read This Assessment

This assessment evaluates how well each system in the Advocate Health landscape can support the prioritized Data 360 use cases — Patient Booking Completion, Genesys Integration, Messaging Orchestration, Primary Care Triggers, and Health Quizzes — and, more broadly, whether the organization can reliably identify and understand a single person across every role they hold (patient, member, donor, provider, employee, caregiver, prospect). Each system is scored 1–5 on eight weighted dimensions; the weighted total (also shown out of 100) determines its readiness label and rank.

The 1–5 scoring scale

1
Not sufficient
Not sufficient to support the use cases.
2
Significant gaps
Significant gaps limit reliability and usability.
3
Partial readiness
Supports use cases with constraints or additional effort.
4
Strong support
Strong support with minor gaps.
5
Fully ready
Well-positioned to support use cases consistently and at scale.

The eight dimensions

Two dimensions are computed from underlying sub-scores; the other six are scored directly from discovery evidence. Weights sum to 100% and reflect each dimension's importance to Data 360 readiness.

Initiative Contribution (REWIRE 2030 value) Weight: Separate score Computed
How relevant the system is to Advocate Health's prioritized Persona × Initiative combinations (the enterprise strategy grid). This is the strategic-value lens: it answers "how much does this system matter to the initiatives we care about?"
Reading the score: A high score means the system serves many high-priority audience/initiative combinations (e.g. Patients × Pioneer National Service Lines). A low score means it is niche or peripheral to the prioritized strategy — not that the system is unimportant operationally.
How it's calculated: Derived from the 9-persona × 6-initiative relevance matrix: each cell is rated 0-5 for how relevant this system is to that persona/initiative, then weighted by the business priority of each cell and normalized to a 1-5 score.
Identity Data Quality Weight: 21% Computed
How reliably a person can be identified using the data within the system. This is the single most heavily weighted dimension because identity resolution is the foundation of Data 360.
Reading the score: A high score means people are consistently and uniquely identifiable and can be matched across systems. A low score means duplicates, missing identifiers, or inconsistent entry make it hard to know who is who.
How it's calculated: Computed from every identifier the system holds (e.g. Name+DOB, Email, MRN), each scored on four equally-weighted factors — Coverage, Consistency, Uniqueness, Matchability — then averaged.
Data Readiness Weight: 16% Directly scored
Whether the required data exists and is usable for the prioritized use cases.
Reading the score: A high score means core data is complete, documented, and well-aligned to the target model. A low score means data is missing, undocumented, or has significant quality issues that require cleanup before use.
Event Readiness Weight: 12% Directly scored
Whether the system captures relevant events (appointments, calls, form submissions, booking abandonment) and makes them available in a timely manner.
Reading the score: A high score means events are captured in or near real time. A low score means batch-only capture, long delays, or missing event types that block trigger-based engagement.
Data Accessibility Weight: 12% Directly scored
Whether data can be accessed and extracted when needed, including near-real-time scenarios.
Reading the score: A high score means well-documented APIs and standard connectors. A low score means manual exports only, restrictive access, or no programmatic path.
Consent Maturity Weight: 12% Directly scored
The consistency, structure, and enforceability of consent data, including alignment to Salesforce privacy models.
Reading the score: A high score means a standardized, auditable, channel-aware consent model that is consistently enforced. A low score means no consent model, or a single global flag with no history — a compliance risk for outreach.
Data Scale & Velocity Weight: 12% Directly scored
Whether the record volume and update frequency align with Data 360 ingestion limits and consumption tiers.
Reading the score: A high score means high volume with frequent/near-real-time updates that fit ingestion expectations. A low score means either negligible volume (little value) or volume/velocity that is not understood or hard to ingest.
Downstream Readiness Weight: 16% Directly scored
Whether the system can receive and act on Data 360 outputs ("insights"), e.g. via custom field capacity for Data Actions.
Reading the score: A high score means the system can consume Data 360 outputs via standard connectors. A low score means it is standalone and cannot accept inbound updates without new architecture.

How identity is scored

Identity Data Quality is built from every person-identifier a system holds. Each identifier is rated on four equally-weighted factors:

  • Coverage — Whether the identifier is consistently populated (what % of records have it).
  • Consistency — Whether it is entered/used the same way every time within the system.
  • Uniqueness — Whether it actually distinguishes one person from another.
  • Matchability — Whether it can be used to link the same person's records across systems.

The Persona × Initiative matrix

Initiative Contribution comes from rating each system against a grid of 9 personas (Patients, Research, Education, Enterprise, B2B, Job Seekers, Clinicians, Payers, Non Patient) and 6 enterprise initiatives (Build the Preeminent National Academic Model; Propel Innovation – Partnership Evolutions; Curate a System of Systems; Pioneer National Service Lines; Health Equity; Harmonize). Each persona/initiative cell carries a business-priority weight, so a system that serves high-priority combinations scores higher than one that serves only low-priority ones.

Two scores: readiness and value

Each system gets two separate scores. Readiness answers "can it connect to Data 360?" It is a weighted average of the seven technical areas, using the weights shown above (rounded). REWIRE 2030 value answers "should it connect?" It comes from the Initiative Contribution matrix and reads High (3.5 or more), Medium (2.5 to 3.5), Low (under 2.5) or Not relevant. Together they drive each system's roadmap placement: whether it should connect to Data 360, and if so, when. A system can be ready but low in value, or high in value with gaps to close first.

Salesforce orgs score Data Accessibility and Downstream Readiness on the native Data 360 connector and activation, not on what each org connects to today. The effort to set up those connections shows in the roadmap.

How to read each section of this dashboard

  • Ranked Scorecard — every system ordered by readiness score, with its REWIRE 2030 value beside it. Start here for the "who's ready" answer. The Top Gap column names each system's lowest-scoring dimension — the first thing to fix.
  • Dimension Analysis — the radar compares the shape of each system's strengths/weaknesses across all eight dimensions; the bar chart ranks overall scores. Use the radar to spot patterns (e.g. consent is weak estate-wide).
  • Assessment Progress — what has been assessed, what is queued, and what is out of scope.
  • System Detail Cards — the evidence behind every score, written for both audiences (see below), plus key observations and recommendations.
  • Roadmap: when each system is planned to be assessed, connected to Data 360, merged or retired, along with program milestones, each system's Data 360 potential and value for REWIRE 2030, and changes to watch. Dates are planning targets to confirm with each system owner.

If you're a business stakeholder

Focus on the label (Strong support / Partial readiness / Significant gaps), the Top Gap, and the Key Observations & Recommendations on each card. These tell you what a system can do for patients/members today and what has to change before it can be relied on — without needing the technical detail.

If you're a technical stakeholder

Read the per-dimension evidence on each card: it names the integrations, identifiers, record volumes, sync cadences, and constraints behind the number. Each dimension's evidence is written as "Business: … Technical: …" so you can jump straight to the architecture-relevant part and the Next Steps.
